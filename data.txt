The provided document details the historical and religious significance of the **Shree Reda Samadhi Temple** located in Ale, Maharashtra. It outlines the miraculous story of the buffalo (Reda) that chanted the Vedas, the subsequent establishment of its Samadhi, and the ongoing development of the temple complex.

* [cite_start]**Location**: Shrikhetra Ale, Santwadi, Kolwadi, Taluka Junnar, District Pune, Pin Code 412 411[cite: 4, 13, 26].

## **Historical and Miraculous Background**
The temple is centered around a miracle performed by the saint **Sant Dnyaneshwar Maharaj**:
* [cite_start]**The Challenge**: In Paithan (circa 1287 AD), religious scholars (Pandits) challenged Dnyaneshwar, questioning if the same divine soul existed in both humans and animals[cite: 37, 41, 44, 49].
* [cite_start]**The Miracle**: Dnyaneshwar placed his hand on the head of a buffalo (owned by a fisherman named Vakoba) and commanded it to chant the Vedas[cite: 39, 45]. [cite_start]The buffalo began reciting the Rigveda perfectly[cite: 46].
* [cite_start]**The Samadhi**: After this event, the buffalo became Dnyaneshwar’s first disciple[cite: 47]. [cite_start]On **Magh Vadya 3, Shake 1212 (1290 AD)**, the buffalo chose to leave its body at Ale[cite: 56, 57]. [cite_start]Dnyaneshwar and his siblings personally established the Samadhi at this site[cite: 57, 58, 60].

## **Religious Significance and Traditions**
* [cite_start]**Uniqueness**: It is claimed to be the only Samadhi of an animal on Earth established by the hands of four great saints: Dnyaneshwar, Nivruttinath, Sopankaka, and Muktabai[cite: 58, 59].
* [cite_start]**Wari Tradition**: For pilgrims who miss the annual Wari to Pandharpur or Alandi, visiting Ale is believed to grant the same religious merit[cite: 69].
* [cite_start]**Key Deities**: The temple houses the Samadhi stone, along with idols of Vitthal-Rukmini and Sant Dnyaneshwar (consecrated on March 5, 1955)[cite: 60, 69, 70].
* **Festivals**:
    * [cite_start]**Mahashivratri**: Includes a week-long *Harinam Saptah* and a flower shower ceremony at the Samadhi at 12:30 PM[cite: 74].
    * [cite_start]**Chaitra Yatra**: A three-day festival from Chaitra Vadya Ekadashi to Trayodashi featuring kirtans, wrestling matches (*Kusti*), and the breaking of a *Dahi Handi*[cite: 76, 78, 79].
    * [cite_start]**Monthly Observances**: 27 nearby villages take turns managing food distribution (*Annadan*) and kirtans on Ekadashi and Dwadashi[cite: 71, 72].

## **Infrastructure and Development**
[cite_start]The Devasthan Trust is actively developing the temple complex using government funds and private donations[cite: 83, 85, 87].
* [cite_start]**Ancient Barav**: A historic stepwell built by **Ahilyabai Holkar** is located on the premises[cite: 98].
* **Current Projects**:
    * [cite_start]Construction of a new hand-carved stone **Vinamandap** (pavilion) to replace an older structure[cite: 86, 97].
    * [cite_start]Development of a Mahadwar (main gate), decorative gardens, and lighting[cite: 99, 103, 105].
* [cite_start]**Facilities**: The trust maintains modern guest houses (*Dharmashalas*) in Alandi and Pandharpur for pilgrims[cite: 81].
